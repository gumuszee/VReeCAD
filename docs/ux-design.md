# UX design

The interface of BlocksCAD follows the control scheme of [Open Brush](https://github.com/icosa-foundation/open-brush) (Apache-2.0), adapted to parametric CAD,
with the direct, "hand is the cursor" feel of Open Blocks. Open Brush code and assets are used as a reference for behaviour only; nothing is copied.
The upstream interface (quick menu on the left trigger, stick locomotion) is not part of BlocksCAD.

## Principles

1. **One finger per job.** The thumb rests on the stick; a task never needs the thumb to hold a face button while the stick is used. Selection by hand movement or by the stick alone, confirmation by trigger or stick click.
2. **Hands, not menus.** Tools live on a palette carried by the off hand; the tool hand points at it. Navigation is grabbing the world with the grips.
3. **Everything is a modular panel.** A face is a button (label, action). Faces sit on panels, panels sit on the sides of a carousel around the palette controller, and the layout is data the user can rearrange.
4. **Readable at arm's length.** Fixed physical sizes (face edge 3.6 cm, label glyphs of the panels about 7 mm, other labels about 9 mm), high contrast, backing plates, one sans serif font (Segoe UI Bold) for every label; no overlapping text.
5. **Feedback on every state change.** Haptic tick on hover change and activation, highlight on hover, a short toast for results.
6. **Hardware independent.** Only the five abstract inputs per hand plus the optional `aux1` and `aux2` (`input-profiles.md`); poses decide the rest.
7. **CAD, not painting.** The model is worked on from every angle, so the view rotates freely; levelling is the modifier, not the default.

## Hand roles

| Role | Default | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| Tool hand | right | the active tool (tip, ray, trigger = primary action), the navigation modifiers |
| Palette hand | left | the palette carousel, undo and redo shortcuts |

Handedness is a preference; the roles swap as a whole. The **Tool hand** face of the Settings panel swaps them (a toast says "Tool hand: left" or "Tool hand: right"): everything that belongs to a hand follows at once, the carousel and the pointer rules of the palette, the quick radial, the option disc, the undo button, the value panels, the navigation modifiers and the tools themselves (`UiController.set_tool_hand`), and the choice is saved in `settings.json` (`tool_hand`) and applied when the viewer starts. The D-pad shortcuts for undo and redo belong to the palette hand, so with the tool hand on the left they are not available (a Steam Frame has them on its left controller only); `button2` of the tool hand undoes on every controller.

## Navigation (grips)

Derived from Open Brush's world transform, with a free orbit:

- **One grip on empty space:** the world is held in the hand: it follows the position and the rotation of the hand and turns about the hand.
- **Both grips:** two-point transform. The pivot is the midpoint between the hands; the world scales by the ratio of the hand distance, rotates with the line between the hands and moves with the midpoint. Scale is clamped to 1:100 .. 100:1.
- **Free orbit (default):** with one grip the world takes the full rotation of the hand; with two grips it takes the shortest rotation that turns the old hand line into the new one. The model tips over in every direction.
- **Level (hold button1 of the tool hand, A on the Steam Frame):** the rotation is limited to the vertical axis (one grip: the yaw of the hand), so the bottom of the model stays down. Pressing or releasing the modifier mid gesture starts a new gesture from the current pose; nothing jumps. The mode is shown on the plate.
- **Hold 1:1 (hold button2 of the tool hand, B on the Steam Frame, while gripping):** the scale animates to 1 : 1 about the hands (the world point between the hands stays between them) and stays at 1 : 1 for as long as the button is held, however the hands move; the world still follows them. Releasing the button lets the hand distance scale the world again, starting from 1 : 1.
- **Reset (stick click of a gripping hand, or the Reset view face):** animates to the home pose: scale 1 : 1, the document origin 1 m in front of the head (horizontally), at 75 % of the head height above the floor (chest height when standing), turned so that the front view of the document (looking along the document Y axis) points away from the head.
- **Tip mode:** the world is held with the tips instead of the grips. One grip pivots the world about the tip (the point under the tip stays under it while the hand turns), two grips scale and rotate about the midpoint between the tips, and the ruler runs between the tips, so the tips measure: touch two points of the model and read the distance.
- **After a two-grip gesture** the grips rarely let go at the same moment. When one grip is released first, the other does nothing until both are released (the model does not drift while the hands let go one after the other); a new grip after both are released works as usual, and a second grip pressed while the first is still held resumes two-hand navigation.
- While a grip navigates, the carousel hides and the tool is inactive. A haptic tick marks every doubling or halving of the scale.
- A grip pressed on an object grabs that object instead (milestone M2).

### Scale ruler

While both grips hold the world, a line joins the hands and reads as a ruler in the measurement system of the document:

- **Ticks** stand at round lengths of the model as it is at the current scale (1, 2, 5 times a power of ten of the unit), about 7 cm apart on the stage, with a labelled tick at every step and unlabelled ticks between them. The ruler starts at 0 at the left hand.
- **Units** follow the `UnitSystem` property of the active document: metric systems label in millimetres, centimetres (Building Euro), metres and kilometres, and lengths under a millimetre as decimals of a millimetre (0.25 mm) or as micrometres (250 µm), as the **Small lengths** face of the Settings panel says (decimals by default; the setting `sub_mm` keeps the choice); imperial systems in thou, inches, feet and miles. The readout of a value shows the digits of its step (a step of 0.1 mm shows 123.4 mm).
- **Summary plate** below the line: the scale ratio (`4 : 1` when the model is enlarged, `1 : 4` when shrunk), the length between the hands in the document unit, and the modes (free orbit or level, and 1 : 1 locked).
- **One grip** has no span to measure: the scale ratio and the modes float above the gripping hand.
- The ruler is drawn on top of everything (no depth test) with a dark outline, so the model never hides it and it reads on light and dark backgrounds. It uses the interface font.

## Palette carousel (palette hand)

The carousel is a prism around the long axis of the palette controller. It is attached to the controller: it follows the position and the rotation of the hand (Open Brush's wand panels).

- **Geometry.** The axis runs along the controller from the grip point forward (the carousel centre is 9 cm in front of the grip point). The side at the front stands on the thumb side of the controller, facing up when the controller is held upright, with its top edge pointing the way the controller points. The other sides stand around the axis at equal angles, and **all sides are visible at all times**. Rolling the wrist rolls the carousel.
- **Panels** are pages of faces (4 columns by 3 rows, face edge 3.6 cm, a panel is 17.3 cm wide and 15.2 cm high) with a title bar and nothing else, so no panel space is spent on indicators. A side carries rows of panels: panels next to each other in a row, rows above each other (the first row nearest the user, further rows further forward). The prism radius grows with the number of sides and the width of the panels so that sides do not overlap.
- **Stick left/right turns the carousel** one side per flick (snap with a haptic tick and a short ease). No button is held. Sides that face away from the user are drawn but cannot be pointed at.
- **Visibility:** the carousel fades in when the head looks within 35 degrees of the controller or of the centre of any panel (the controller at 0.12 to 0.95 m from the head) and, once shown, stays until the gaze is 48 degrees away from them, so a long column of panels, the value panels, is kept while its far end is looked at. While the pointer works on the carousel (hovering a face or a title, a press, a slider drag) or the stick of the tool hand steps a value, the limit is 75 degrees. It fades out otherwise and is hidden while gripping. Palette hand `button1` pins it visible or releases it.
- **Two carousels:** the carousel holds the palette (the tools and the other panels of the user's layout). While an editable object is selected, the **values carousel** (see Value panels) can take its place, and the trigger of the palette hand swaps them.
- **Pointing:** the tool hand points with its laser or its tip (see Pointer below). The face under the pointer highlights and a haptic tick marks the change. A face that is hovered or active, and a slider that is hovered, dragged or has its bar hot, stands 5 mm in front of its panel (its text, and the track and handle of a slider, with it). A face is pressed with the trigger and activates when the trigger is released over the same face within 0.4 s; a pointer that leaves the face before the release cancels the press, and a longer hold lifts the face (see Moving and hiding faces).

### Panels (initial)

| Panel | Faces |
|---|---|
| Tools | Create, Select, Move, Push/Pull, Points, Sketch |
| Edit | Undo, Redo, Delete |
| Modify | Fuse, Cut, Common, Copy, Mirror X, Mirror Y, Mirror Z, Colour, Pattern X, Pattern Y, Pattern Z |
| Shape | Extrude, Fillet, Chamfer, Attach, Detach, Fixed joint, Hinge, Slider, Ball joint, Unjoint, Sources |
| View | Reset view, Reload scene, Reset layout, Show hidden, Close VR |
| Pointer | Pointer (laser or tip), and the sliders Angle and Length (tip angle and tip length) |
| Settings | Tool hand, Small lengths, Tutorial, Snapping (guides or inference), and the sliders Grid (the grid step of every tool: 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 or 100 mm, default 10 mm) and Angle step (the angle step of a locked turn: 5, 15, 30, 45 or 90 degrees, default 15) |

**Delete** removes the selected objects in one transaction, so one undo step brings them back; a face, an edge or a vertex that is selected is not an object and is left alone (a toast says so). The selection that remains is what was not deleted.

**Modify** works on the selected whole objects (selected faces, edges and vertices are not objects and are left alone, a toast says so; the order of the selection matters):

- **Fuse** joins two or more objects into one `Part::MultiFuse`, **Common** keeps what they all share (`Part::MultiCommon`) and **Cut** takes every object selected after the first away from the first (`Part::Cut`; several tools are fused first, in a `Part::MultiFuse` of their own). The result is a real feature that refers to its sources, so changing a source (a slider, the Move tool, the keypad) changes the result. The sources are hidden, not changed, and the result becomes the selection. A boolean that leaves nothing (objects that do not touch, a cut that removes everything) is refused with a toast and leaves the document as it was.
- **Copy** copies each selected object together with the features it is built from and lays the copy beside the original, one length of its box along X plus 10 mm away; the copy is a feature of its own with its own properties (a copied box has its own Length), never a frozen shape. The copies become the selection.
- **Mirror X, Y, Z** mirrors each selected object across a plane that stands 5 mm beyond its box along that axis, so the image lies 10 mm from the object; the image is a `Part::Mirroring` whose `Base` and `Normal` hold the plane (the plane does not move when the source changes size; the image follows the source). The mirror images become the selection.
- **Pattern X, Y, Z** repeats each selected object three times along that axis, one box length plus 10 mm apart, as a Draft array (`NumberX` and `IntervalX` and the like are properties of the array; the original is the first of the three and is hidden as a source). The arrays become the selection.
- **Extrude** extrudes every selected sketch by 20 mm along its normal into a solid (a `Part::Extrusion` of the sketch, so the solid follows the sketch and its length is a value of the new solid: the Value panel shows it as **Length**). The sketch is hidden and the solids become the selection. A sketch whose outline is open at exactly two ends (an arc, or a chain of lines and arcs that has not come back to its start) is closed first with a line between the ends, joined to them by constraints, in the same undo step, and the toast says so ("Extruded Sketch, closed with a line"); a single line (the line between its ends is there already), a chain with more than two open ends and a sketch without a closed outline otherwise are refused with a toast and nothing is made.
- **Fillet** and **Chamfer** (panel Shape, with Extrude) work on selected edges, not on whole objects (select them with the Select tool, target Edge): each object that has edges selected gets one `Part::Fillet` or `Part::Chamfer` of those edges, 5 mm to begin with, and is hidden as a source. The size is the one value of the new feature, shown on the Value panel as **Radius** or **Length** (the value slider, the stick and the keypad edit it; it cannot be bound to a variable, because a row of the `Edges` property takes no expression). A size that the edges cannot take (larger than they allow) is replaced, when the feature is made, by the largest size that they can take (a multiple of 0.1 mm), and the toast names it ("Fillet: 2 edges, 3.5 mm (5 mm is too large)"); a size typed or dragged on the panels for an existing feature is refused with a toast that names the largest size ("The edges cannot take that size, the largest is 3.5 mm") and the feature keeps its size. The faces that the new features add to their sources (the rounded or cut faces, found as the faces that lie on no surface of the source) become the selection, so the panels follow the feature and only its new faces are highlighted; a feature without such a face is selected as a whole.
- **Attach** takes the first two selected faces (select them with the Select tool, target Face, Add): the object of the first face moves, the second face is the target. Both faces must be flat and on different objects. The moving object gets `AttachmentSupport` = the target face, `MapMode` = FlatFace and an `AttachmentOffset` that lays the two faces against each other with their centres together, so FreeCAD's Attacher computes the placement and the object follows its support when that is moved. Attaching an object to something that is built from it is refused with a toast. Moving, turning or typing the position of an attached object (Move tool, value panels) changes the `AttachmentOffset`, so it stays attached; its position cannot be bound to a variable. The moving object becomes the selection.
- **Sources** selects the objects that the selected features are made from (the objects a boolean, a fillet, a chamfer, an extrusion, a mirror or an array refers to, the base of a cut before its tools), also when they are hidden, as the whole objects; a selected face, edge or vertex counts for its object. The panels then follow the first source, so its sizes and position are values to edit, and the feature follows. Hidden sources are drawn by the selection highlight; the toast names them and counts the hidden ones ("Sources: Box, Cylinder (2 hidden)"). Pressing it again with sources selected opens their own sources, a selection without sources says so and is left as it is. Objects that have a shape of their own count; spreadsheets and origins do not. One press is no undo step (the selection is not part of the document).
- **Detach** turns the attachment of the selected objects off and keeps them where they are.
- **Fixed joint**, **Hinge**, **Slider** and **Ball joint** connect two selected faces like Attach does (the first face moves, the second is the target, both on different objects) but through the Assembly workbench: the objects become children of an `Assembly::AssemblyObject` (made with the first joint, one per document), the target is grounded (a `GroundedJoint`) unless it is held already, and a joint of the Assembly type Fixed, Revolute, Slider or Ball joins the face centres. A fixed joint lays the faces against each other, a hinge turns about the axis through the face centres, a slider slides along it, a ball joint turns freely about the centres. After every move or typed position of a part of an assembly (Move tool, value panels) the solver of the assembly (`AssemblyObject.solve`) brings it back to a position its joints allow, so a hinged part can be pushed around but only turns about its pin; the solver takes the position of the hand as a first guess and does not follow it exactly (a hinge turned by 40 degrees may end up at a different angle). An attached object cannot be jointed (Detach first). **Unjoint** deletes the joints of the selected objects; the objects stay in the assembly where they are.
- **Colour** gives every selected object (whole or by an element) the next colour of a palette of eight (grey, red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet, white), each from its own current colour; the toast names the colour. The colour is kept in the property `BlocksCADColour` of the object (the view of the object follows it, also after an undo or a redo), so each press is one undo step.
- Every other action is one undo step.

Planned: mirror about a chosen plane, a pattern with a typed count and interval, Settings (comfort, snapping).

### Guides

The Create tool, the Points tool, the Sketch tool, the Push/Pull tool and the Move tool line a point up with the model while it is placed or dragged (`core/guides.py`, `cad/anchors.py`). The anchors are the vertices and the middles of the straight edges of the visible objects (the block being dragged by the Points tool is left out; at most 3000 anchors, read again every half second). Within 8 mm of hand space (8 mm at 1 : 1) an anchor attracts a coordinate: on every axis where an anchor has nearly the cursor's coordinate the point takes that coordinate, the other axes stay on the grid; a line in the colour of the axis (X red, Y green, Z blue) runs from the anchor to the point and a cross marks the anchor, and when one anchor matches on all three axes the point is that anchor (an orange cross). The hand ticks when a guide appears where there was none (the same weak tick as a grid step; a change from one guide to another is silent). The ticks of the tools, the sliders and the stick (a grid step, a guide, a slider step) are weak pulses and a hand gets at most one every 0.15 s; the Move tool ticks for every grid step the object travels, locked to the grid or free. Guides are drawn in the scene (bright where nothing is in front, dim behind the model), never over everything. With the Grid or Free placement in the Points tool the point has no guides (see Placement and inference). The Sketch tool lines the points of a shape up on the plane of the sketch: every anchor of the model is seen on that plane (its position along the plane, whatever its height above it), and the points of the sketch itself are anchors too (read live; a point that is dragged does not line up with itself or with the points that move with it). On the plane two coordinates line up, and an anchor that matches both is a point guide. Both ends of a line, rectangle or circle and the cursor of a new sketch use the guides (the cursor of a sketch that is attached to a face does not, the press does); with the Free placement the end of a shape, the start of a shape and a dragged point are free, and with the Grid placement they are on the grid without guides. A guide runs in the colour of the axis of the document that it follows, and in a light grey where the plane of the sketch has no axis of the document along it (a face that is tilted). The Push/Pull tool lines the face being moved up level with the points of the model: when the plane of the face, at the distance the hand gives, is within 8 mm of hand space of the plane through an anchor (the object being changed is not an anchor, nor is a point already in the plane of the face), the distance is the one that puts the face in that plane, off the grid, a guide shows (a cross on the anchor and a line to the hand's place in the plane of the face, in the colour of the axis along the normal of the face, light grey for a tilted face) and the readout names the distance. The Move tool lines the object being moved up with the model while the grab is free: the points of the object are the middle and the eight corners of the box around it and its vertices (at most 64, in its own frame, turned with it), and on each axis the point that is nearest to an anchor (the object itself is none) within the reach is brought level with it, by shifting the object along that axis, so a face or a corner of the object ends level with a face or a corner of another one; a line in the colour of the axis runs from the anchor to the point, the hand ticks when a guide appears, and a point that is level with one anchor on all three axes gets the orange cross. With the Grid or Free placement there are no guides; while the object is scaled there are none either. While a **line** is drawn in the Sketch tool its end also lines up in direction: when the end is within the reach of the ray from the start that runs parallel or perpendicular to a slanted line of the sketch (a line along neither axis of the sketch), the end moves to the nearest point of that ray and a light grey guide runs from the middle of that line to the middle of the new one, labelled "Parallel" or "Perpendicular" (the nearest relation wins; with the A button held there is none). The end is then exactly so, which is what makes the new line get the **parallel** or **perpendicular** constraint when it is drawn (see Constraints). The length of the line lines up too: when it is within the reach of the length of another line of the sketch (any line, the sides of a rectangle included) the end moves along the line to exactly that length and a light grey guide labelled "Equal length" runs from the middle of that line to the middle of the new one (the nearest length wins; after the direction has moved the end, so a line can be both parallel and as long). Such a line gets the **equal** constraint when it is drawn. A guide for tangent is not built.

### Placement and inference

**Placement** says how a tool puts a point, a size or an object: **Snap** (the default: rounded to the grid and lined up with the model by the guides), **Grid** (rounded to the grid only, no guides) or **Free** (exactly where the hand is). It is one setting for every tool (`settings.json`, `placement`). While a tool is in a gesture (the trigger is held on a drag, a grab, a push) the quick radial is closed, so the **A button** (`button1`) of the tool hand changes the placement: each press goes to the next one (Snap, Grid, Free, Snap ...), a toast names it ("Placement: Grid") and the hand ticks. The placement stays for the next gesture. Outside a gesture A is the quick radial as before. In the Move tool Grid is the locked grab (the move in whole grid steps, the turn in whole angle steps, a scale to the grid) and Free and Snap are the free grab (Snap lines the object up with the model); in the Points, Sketch, Create and Push/Pull tools Free leaves the point, the end of a shape or the distance as the hand has it, and in the Sketch tool the direction and length relations of a new line (parallel, perpendicular, equal) belong to Snap.

**Inference** is the alternative source of the guides, chosen with the **Snapping** face of the Settings panel (Guides or Inference; `snap_source`) so the two can be compared. **Guides** line a point up with every point of the model (see Guides), which pulls the point all the time in a scene with many objects. **Inference** offers the guides only the points near what the hand works on: those within 6 cm of hand space of the cursor (of the box of the object in the Move tool), and the points that the hand has **acquired**: a point that the cursor has stayed within 1.2 cm of for 0.4 s (the hand ticks, a cyan cross marks it) stays offered, 3 at most, the oldest goes first, until the hand has done nothing that asks for guides for a second. So a far point is lined up with by dwelling on it first. The module is `core/inference.py` and its only caller is the guide service of the host (`guide_anchors` in `commonXR.py`), so dropping the setting means dropping that branch and the module.

### Fixing

A fixed thing is held in place by a constraint: the tools do not move it, and what it is joined to cannot carry it along. While a tool is in a gesture (the trigger is held on something) the **B button** (`button2`) of the tool hand **fixes** what the gesture holds, or **releases** it when it is fixed already; outside a gesture B is undo (tap) and redo (hold) as before, and during a gesture it is no undo. A toast names the result ("Fixed Box", "Released point 6"), the hand ticks, and the change is part of the undo step of the gesture (a grip that cancels the gesture takes it back too). A fixed thing that is pressed again does not follow the hand ("Fixed: Box (B releases)" on the readout); B releases it and it follows from where the hand is at that moment.

- **Body (Move tool):** the grabbed object gets the property `BlocksCADFixed`. A fixed body is not moved or turned or scaled, and neither is a body that a fixed body is attached to, directly or through other attached bodies (`AttachmentSupport`): moving the support would carry the fixed body, so the fixed one holds the support too, and the readout names it ("Other is fixed to it (B releases it)"). Releasing the fixed body frees both.
- **Point of a block (Points tool):** the point is added to the property `FixedPoints` of the block. The tool does not move a fixed point and does not remove it ("Point 6 is fixed: press B while holding it to release it"); the other points of the block move as usual, and a point that is removed renumbers the fixed points after it. The block as a whole can still be moved by the Move tool.
- **Point of a sketch (Sketch tool, Edit mode):** the point is fixed by two Sketcher constraints (DistanceX and DistanceY from the origin, named `lock_<geometry>_<point>_x` and `_y`, which are no values of the panels). The solver keeps the point where it is while other points are dragged or dimensions change, so the corner of a rectangle that is fixed stays while the opposite corner is dragged; a point that the solver cannot fix ("The sketch cannot fix that point") is left alone. Releasing deletes the two constraints.
- Faces, edges and whole sketch curves are not fixed (nothing holds them in a gesture), and a fixed state is not drawn on the model: the toast and the readout say it.

### Overlapping faces

Faces of two objects that lie in one plane (a box standing on another, a cut that ends in the plane of the base) are drawn at the same depth, so the depth test chooses one of them per pixel, differently from frame to frame: a flicker, most visible where the objects have different colours or one is selected. While the viewer runs every object of the active document is pushed back by a polygon offset that depends on the order in which the objects were first seen (`render/depth_order.py`): 16 levels, the newest level is not pushed, older ones a few more steps of the depth buffer (micrometres) with the slope part growing too, so a shared plane always shows the newer object. Offsets only push back, so the edges of an object, which are drawn without one, stay in front of its faces. The view provider of a Part object has a polygon offset node of its own (between its points and its faces; a node later in the root replaces an earlier one), whose values are shifted and checked twice a second; a view provider without one gets a node of our own. New objects get theirs within half a second; everything is put back when the viewer stops or another document becomes active, since the scene graph is the one of the FreeCAD view. Two objects that are 16 places apart share an offset and can still flicker.

### Sliders

A slider is a face that takes a whole row of its panel: the label with the current value on the left, a track (the **bar**) in the lower part of the row and a handle. **Every slider behaves the same way.** It is **focused** when the pointer comes close without pressing: the tip within 10 cm in front of the panel over the row, or the laser over the row. It is **dragged** only from its bar: point at the bar (laser) or touch it (tip), the bar turns white, **hold the trigger** and move the pointer along the track (a press on the label part of the row does nothing): the handle follows the pointer projected onto the panel, also beyond the ends of the panel (the value stops at the ends), and the value changes in steps with a small haptic tick on every step. Releasing the trigger ends the drag and saves the setting. A slider goes in the first slot of a row; the other slots of that row stay empty. The Pointer panel carries the pointer sliders, the Settings panel the grid and angle step sliders and the value panels carry the sliders of the values of the selected object; the focus of a value slider is what lets the stick step its value (see Value panels).

### Rearranging panels

Each panel has a title bar with a grip mark on its right (a tap on the mark opens the panel editor, see Panel editor). The layout of the palette carousel is a JSON document (`%APPDATA%\BlocksCAD\layout.json`, version 5, with the optional hue of a panel; the values carousel keeps its own in `values_layout.json`, see Value panels): the panels in order, the side and the row of each panel, the face ids in each slot and the ids of the hidden faces. Defaults come from code. The initial layout has one panel per side.

1. Point the tool hand at a title bar (it highlights) and **hold the trigger**: the panel lifts off the carousel and follows the laser as a small card. The panels close up and one extra empty side ("Drop here") is offered at the end of the carousel.
2. **Turn the carousel** with the palette stick while holding, to bring the target side to the front (only sides facing the user can be pointed at).
3. **Aim at a panel** to choose where the lifted panel goes; an orange bar shows the place and the panel under the laser lights up:
   - the left or right half of the panel: next to it in its row, in front of it (left) or after it (right);
   - the upper or lower quarter of the panel: a new row above or below it;
   - the empty slot of an empty side: that side, alone.
4. **Release the trigger** to drop the panel. Releasing over nothing cancels.
5. The layout is saved on every change. Stacking panels in rows and columns on one side puts every module in reach without turning the carousel. The **Reset layout** face restores the defaults of both carousels and removes the saved files.

### Panel editor

A **tap on the menu mark** (the three grooves, the hamburger) at the right end of the title bar of a panel of the palette carousel opens the panel editor, which renames the panel and gives it a colour. The mark lights up under the pointer; a press on it does not lift the panel (the rest of the title bar does) and the editor opens when the trigger is released over the same mark. OpenXR has no keyboard (the core specification, the EXT and Valve extensions have none; the one that exists, `XR_META_virtual_keyboard`, belongs to Meta runtimes), so the editor is a carousel of its own made of the same panels, `ui/panel_editor.py`:

- **Keyboard:** three panels standing next to each other in the bottom row, a wide keyboard of 12 columns and 3 rows that reads across the panels (A to L, M to X, Y to 9). Letters are capitals at the start of the name and after a space, and small letters otherwise; the **Case** key keeps the capitals on.
- **Name** (above the keyboard, left): the text field (the readout, with a cursor), **Space**, **Delete**, **Clear**, **Case**, **OK** and **Cancel**. A name has at most 16 characters (it has to fit the title bar) and needs a letter; the text starts as the name the panel has.
- **Colour** (next to Name): **Auto** (the colour that the name gives the panel) and eleven hues. Every panel of the editor wears the colour that is chosen, so it is seen before OK.

**OK** gives the panel the name and the colour, saves the layout and brings the carousel that was shown back, **Cancel** leaves the panel as it is. While the editor is open nothing on it can be lifted, the trigger of the palette hand does not swap carousels, and a selection that comes up waits: the values carousel is shown after the editor closes. The panels of the values carousel and the Value panel have no editor (their names are what the program finds them by). The name and the colour (`hue`) of a panel are part of the layout file (`layout.json`, version 5); a layout file of an older version is migrated to the default panels when it is read, and one of version 5 is left as the user made it. The same keyboard can serve other text later (naming a variable); nothing else uses it.

### Moving and hiding faces

A single face is lifted the same way as a panel, with the trigger held on it:

1. Point at a face and **hold the trigger for 0.4 s**: a haptic pulse marks the lift, the face lifts off its slot as a card that follows the pointer, and its slot dims. Every panel shows a **hide slot**, a green square with an X in its title bar (left of the grip mark), for as long as the face is lifted; the carousel keeps its shape.
2. **Aim at a slot** of any panel on the sides facing the user (turn the carousel with the palette stick while holding, as for panels): the slot lights orange. A face already in the slot changes places with the lifted face; an empty slot takes it. Slots in a row that belongs to a slider are not offered, and the slot the face came from is not a target.
3. **Release the trigger** to drop the face. Releasing over nothing, over the source slot or over a refused slot cancels. Dropped on the hide slot of any panel (it turns blue under the pointer), the face is hidden: it leaves the panels and the quick radial.
4. The layout is saved on every change. The **Show hidden** face (View panel) puts every hidden face back into a free slot, on its home panel when that has room; **Reset layout** restores the defaults and unhides everything. Loading a saved layout never places hidden faces again.

Sliders are dragged with the trigger, so they cannot be lifted or hidden; they move with their panel.

## Pointer

Every controller has a pointer pose derived from its aim pose and three settings, which the Pointer panel changes and `%APPDATA%\BlocksCAD\settings.json` keeps:

- **Mode: laser or tip.** Both modes can be compared and switched at any time with the Pointer face.
- **Angle:** the pointer is tilted down from the controller axis by this angle (slider from -30 to 60 degrees in steps of 1, default 15). It follows the roll of the controller.
- **Length:** the distance of the tip from the controller (slider from 0 to 20 cm in steps of 5 mm, default 40 mm, shown in the document unit).

**Laser.** A thin beam from the tool hand along the tilted aim direction, drawn on top of the scene and ending in a dot at the point it hits (0.6 m long when it hits nothing). Panels are pointed at with the ray; models are picked with the ray.

**Tip.** Like the brush tip of Open Brush: a thin cone that narrows from the controller to the tip, ending in a small sphere (7 mm), in front of both controllers, at the tip length along the tilted direction, drawn on top of the scene. The tip of the palette hand is hidden while the carousel is shown; both tips stay visible while navigating. The parts of a tip that lie behind or inside a panel or the model are drawn in the inverted colours (the tip is drawn twice: always, in inverted colours, and again in the normal colours where nothing is in front of it), so the colour shows whether the tip is in front of or in contact with what it acts on. The controller models always hide the tips and the laser: they are drawn a second time over them with a depth test against the scene, so a controller hides the pointer exactly where the controller itself is visible (not where something else is in front of the controller). The base of the cone, which starts inside the controller, therefore never shows in the inverted colours. The tip is the selector:

- panels react to what the tip touches: a panel is touched when the tip is within 3 cm in front of its plate (or up to 1.5 cm behind it) and inside it; the sphere lights up while it touches; the trigger presses a face or lifts a panel by its title bar, as with the laser;
- tools pick with the tip: the pointer pose of each hand (the tip position and the tilted orientation) replaces the aim pose for everything the tools do, and a model is picked only within 3 cm in front of the tip, so objects are selected by touching them.

## Tool hand

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Trigger | primary action of the tool; press and release on a face when pointing (laser) or touching (tip) at the carousel to activate it; hold on a face to lift it, on a title bar to lift a panel |
| Grip | grab the world or an object |
| Stick up/down | first option of the tool, stepped, with a live readout (see Tool options) |
| Stick left/right | second option of the tool (cycle shape or mode) |
| Stick click | the tool decides: Create turns to the other page of shapes, Move puts the grid and the angle step back to their defaults, Select puts the target and the operation back to Object and Replace; a tool with two option axes puts the option changed last back to its default |
| Button 1 | held while navigating (one or two grips): level rotation. Otherwise: hold for the quick radial (below) |
| Button 2 | held while navigating: scale locked at 1 : 1. During a gesture: fix or release what is held (see Fixing). Otherwise: undo (tap), redo (hold) |

### Create tool

The first tool of the palette makes parametric primitives (`tools/primitives.py`, `cad/primitives.py`). The tip of the tool hand is the cursor, in laser mode as well as in tip mode (the tip position is the controller position moved by the tip length along the tilted aim).

- **Snap:** the cursor snaps to a grid in document millimetres (options below). A small cross at the snapped point shows where a press would start; it is drawn over the model so it is never lost. A haptic tick marks every step of a drag to another grid point, not a hover.
- **Drag:** the trigger pressed on a grid point starts an axis-aligned box in document axes; moving the hand drags out the opposite corner in 3D. A ghost of the chosen shape fitted into the box and the outline of the box follow the hand. The ghost is a lit translucent body (front faces only, so there is no internal geometry) with outline curves along its characteristic edges (circles, seams, edges): bright where they lie in front of the model, dim where the model hides them. The ghost is a polygonal mesh built from the same sizing rules as the feature (`cad/meshes.py`, a fixed triangulation at every size, a few milliseconds), the feature itself is made by FreeCAD. The outline of the box is orange; and the readout on the wrist names the shape and the three sizes ("Cylinder: 120 mm x 120 mm x 80 mm") in the unit system of the document. Every axis is at least one grid step long.
- **Release** makes the shape as a real FreeCAD feature in one transaction, so one undo step removes it; a toast reports it. A **tap** (the hand stays within its grid cell) places the shape at a default size of four grid steps centred on the cursor.
- **Shapes** fit into the box: Box (the box itself), Cylinder, Cone and Prism (the hexagonal prism) stand on the bottom of the box with the radius of half the smaller horizontal side, Sphere (half the smallest side) and Torus (tube of a fifth of the smaller horizontal side) are centred in it, Wedge is a ramp that rises towards +Y. The features are `Part::Box`, `Part::Cylinder`, `Part::Cone`, `Part::Sphere`, `Part::Torus`, `Part::Wedge` and `Part::Prism` with their dimensions as properties, so the size can be edited afterwards. **Block** is the box as a solid made of points (see Points tool): its eight corners are the parameters, not a size.
- **Options:** the stick opens an option disc of eight sections (see Tool options), one for every shape. From the top, clockwise: Box, Cylinder, Cone, Sphere, Torus, Wedge, Prism and Block. The grid is the one of the Settings panel (1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 and 100 mm; default 10 mm), shared by every tool. The click of the stick does nothing.
- A press that starts on the carousel or the radial does not start a shape; a grip pressed during a drag cancels it, as does choosing another tool. Without an open document a release makes nothing and a toast says so.
- The grid is in document millimetres, whatever the scale of the world; zooming in with the grips and choosing a finer grid give finer shapes.

### Select tool

The second tool of the palette touches and selects (`tools/select.py`, `tools/touch.py`, `core/selecting.py`, `cad/picking.py`, `render/highlight.py`); it is also the item in the middle of the quick radial. The cursor is the tip of the tool hand, as in the Create tool. It changes nothing in the document.

- **Target:** the option that says what is touched: **Object**, **Face**, **Edge** or **Vertex** (default Object). Only elements of that sort are touched, however close another sort is: the nearest face within 30 mm of the stage, edge within 20 mm, vertex within 12 mm; for an object, any element within those reaches or the inside of a solid touches it. The reach is measured on the stage, as in the Move tool. The touched element is highlighted in the blue of the hover (a whole object is filled with all its edges, a face is filled with its outline, an edge is a curve, a vertex a cross) and a haptic tick marks every change. It is the element a press selects. A change of the target drops the hover at once; the hover is steady against a trembling hand as in the Move tool.
- **Operation:** the trigger pressed on the touched element changes the selection, which is FreeCAD's own selection (so what is selected in VR is selected on the desktop). **Replace** (default) makes the element the only selected one, and a press on nothing clears the selection. **Add** appends the element (a press on an element that is selected says so). **Remove** takes it out (removing a whole object removes every element of it; a press on an element that is not selected says so). Every target can be selected many times over, and objects, faces, edges and vertices may be selected together; the first selected object is the one the value panels follow. A toast reports the change ("Selected Box", "Added Box Face6, 2 selected", "Removed Box, 1 selected", "Selection cleared") and the hand gives a short tick. The selection is drawn in orange, over the model like the hover, for every element.
- **Option disc:** eight sections, from the top clockwise: **Object**, **Face**, **Edge**, **Vertex**, **Remove**, **Add**, and two without a function. A target section sets the target. **Remove** and **Add** set the operation and pushing the same section again goes back to Replace. The lit sections show the current target and operation and the middle of the disc names both. The click of the stick puts the target and the operation back to Object and Replace.
- **Value panels:** the panels of the left-hand carousel follow the selection (see Value panels), so selecting an object opens the value editor as it does for the Move tool.
- A press that starts on the carousel or the radial does not select.

### Move tool

The third tool of the palette touches, selects, moves, turns and scales objects (`tools/move.py`, `tools/touch.py`, `cad/picking.py`, `cad/edit.py`, `core/grab.py`, `render/highlight.py`). The cursor is the tip of the tool hand, as in the Create tool.

- **Touch:** the element the cursor touches is highlighted and a haptic tick marks every change. The highlight moves to another element only when that element has stayed under the hand for 0.08 s (0.15 s before it goes away), so a trembling hand does not make it jump between a corner, its edge and its face. What is touched: the nearest **vertex** (within 12 mm of the stage, a cross), else the nearest **edge** (20 mm, a curve), else the nearest **face** (30 mm, a translucent fill with its outline), else the **object** when the cursor is inside a solid (all its faces filled and all its edges). The reach is measured on the stage, so it is a shorter length in the document when the world is enlarged. Hidden objects are not touched. The hover is blue, the selection orange; both are drawn over the model with bright lines where they are in front and dim lines where the model hides them. The highlight lies on the surface it marks, so it is drawn a little in front of it (a polygon offset and a depth range), and the hover in front of the selection: when the touched element is also selected the two never fight. The geometry of the selection is built again only when the selection or the selected shape changes.
- **Tap** (press and release without moving anything) selects the touched element as FreeCAD's own selection, so what is selected in VR is selected on the desktop. A tap on nothing clears the selection.
- **Grab:** the trigger pressed on an element grabs its object in one transaction and selects the whole object at once (it shows as selected from the first press). A tap that moves nothing selects the touched face, edge or vertex on release. The object follows the hand as a rigid body and is **free**: any position and any angle, a turn of the hand turns the object about the cursor (the option Mode switches the turn off). With the **Grid** placement the grab is **locked**: the move of the hand is rounded to whole grid steps and the turn, taken about each of the three axes, to a multiple of the angle step (5, 15, 30, 45 or 90 degrees; default 15), so the numbers stay clean. A **padlock** of about 14 mm lies flat over the A button of the controller model, in the plane of the top of the controller and turning with it, for as long as a grab runs: open while the grab is free, closed and orange while it is locked (the Grid placement). The quick radial, which `button1` opens otherwise, stays closed during a grab. The readout names the move ("Move: 30 mm, 0, 20 mm, turn 90 deg"). Releasing keeps the change (also when A is let go in the same moment) and leaves the object selected; one undo step takes it back. The change is made to the `Placement` of the feature.
- **Scale:** the trigger of the other hand pressed during a grab scales the object through the size properties of its feature (`cad/edit.py`): the factor is the distance between the two cursors divided by their distance when the second trigger went down, and the centre of the object stays in place. The sizes are free; while A is held every size is rounded to the grid and is at least one grid step long. The readout shows the factor. Releasing the second trigger goes back to moving from the pose the object has. Without a grab that trigger is the one of the palette hand that swaps the carousels. Only objects made by the Create tool can be scaled (box, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, wedge and prism); other objects are only moved.
- **Cancel:** a grip pressed during a grab aborts it and the transaction restores the object; choosing another tool does the same.
- **Options:** the option disc (see Tool options). From the top, clockwise: **Move, turn** and **Move only** (the mode), and six sections without a function. The grid and the angle step of a locked turn are the ones of the Settings panel. The click of the stick does nothing.
- A press that starts on the carousel or the radial does not grab. The tool replaces the selection; choose the Select tool to build a selection of several elements.

### Push/Pull tool

The fourth tool of the palette moves a flat face of a solid along its normal (`tools/pushpull.py`, `cad/pushpull.py`, `tools/touch.py`). The cursor is the tip of the tool hand, as in the Select tool, and only faces are touched (the hover and the selection look as in the Select tool).

- **Press** the trigger on a flat face: the face is selected and the gesture starts. A curved face is selected but cannot be pushed (a toast says so).
- **Drag:** the distance the hand moves along the normal of the face, rounded to the grid (1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 or 100 mm; default 10 mm; a distance of less than one step is none), is how far the face moves: out of the solid is a **push**, into it a **pull**. The ghost shows the volume that the face sweeps (a lit translucent body with its outline, as in the Create tool) and the readout names it ("Push: 30 mm", "Pull: 20 mm"). Sideways movement counts for nothing.
- **Release** makes the change in one undo step and selects the result; a release within one step of the start is a tap that only selects the face. A grip cancels. The stick up and down change the grid and its click puts it back to 10 mm.
- **Sizes:** where a size of the object moves the face, that size is changed and nothing is added: the ends of a `Part::Box` (`Length`, `Width` or `Height`; the end at the low side moves the base of the placement with it, a turned box is read along its own axes), the caps of a `Part::Cylinder` or `Part::Cone` (`Height`) and the two ends of the straight extrusion of a sketch (`LengthFwd` for the far end, `LengthRev` for the end on the sketch). A size that is bound to an expression, a change that would leave a size of nothing or less, an extrusion that is tapered, symmetric or not of a sketch, and every other face (the sides of a cylinder cannot be pushed at all) take the features below. One undo step.
- **Features:** otherwise a `PartDesign::SubShapeBinder` follows the face, a `Part::Extrusion` of the binder along the normal has the distance as its `LengthFwd`, and the result is a `Part::MultiFuse` of the solid and the extrusion (push) or a `Part::Cut` of the extrusion from the solid (pull). The solid and the helper features are hidden; changing `LengthFwd` later changes the result. A pull that leaves nothing is refused with a toast and the document is as it was. The faces of a result can be pushed again.

### Points tool

The fifth tool of the palette edits the points of a **block**, a solid made of points (`tools/points.py`, `cad/block.py`, `cad/blockgeom.py`). A block is made with the Create tool (shape **Block**, a box whose eight corners are points) and is a `Part::FeaturePython` with the properties `Vertices` (the points, in the placement of the object) and `Faces` (one string of point indices such as "0 3 2 1" per face, counter-clockwise from outside). The shape is built again from them at every recompute: a flat face is a face, a face that is not flat is built from triangles (a face of up to seven corners is cut in the way that bends the surface least, which joins a corner that sticks out to the corner below it; a larger one by ear clipping), so moving one point never breaks the solid and every block stays a solid; a position that makes no solid (the faces cross each other, the block turns inside out or has no volume) is refused. The block is an ordinary feature: it can be moved, copied, mirrored, patterned, fused and cut like the others, and its shape is stored with the document. The Points tool edits boxes too: every visible box that no other object uses and whose sizes are no expressions is marked like a block, and the press on one of its points or edges turns the box into a block of the same size, label, colour and place as the press starts (one undo step with the move or the split that follows). A press on nothing in a document without such boxes or blocks says how to make one. A point never lands on another point of the block (the readout says so), and a point that splits an edge starts at least a tenth of the edge (1 mm at most) from its ends.

- **Touch:** every point of every visible block is marked with an orange cross, the point under the cursor (the tip of the tool hand, as in the Select tool) is highlighted blue within 12 mm on the stage (in the **Split** mode the edge within 20 mm).
- **Move** (the mode whenever the tool is chosen): the trigger pressed on a point drags it, but the point stays where it is until the hand has moved 12 mm of stage away from the place of the press (a tap, or a trembling hand, only picks the point and does not snap it to the grid); from then on it follows the cursor rounded to the grid (1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 or 100 mm; default 10 mm), **A held leaves it free**. The block is built again at every step, so the solid follows the hand; a position that makes no solid is refused, the readout says why and the point stays at the last good place. The readout names the point and its position ("Point 6: 520 mm, 100 mm, 140 mm"). Releasing keeps the change in one undo step and selects the block, so the value panels follow it; a release that moved nothing keeps nothing. A grip cancels.
- **Split:** the trigger pressed on an edge adds a point on it (every line on the solid counts: also the lines that cut a face that is not flat into triangles, which first become edges of two faces), where the cursor is nearest, to the two faces that share the edge, and the drag goes on with the new point as in Move. The solid does not change until the new point is moved. The split and the move are one undo step.
- **Remove:** the trigger pressed on a point takes it out of the block in one undo step and selects the block (no drag follows; the mode stays, so several points can go in a row). The faces that have the point lose that corner (a triangle disappears) and the corners next to the point become a face of their own that closes the hole: a point that split an edge only vanishes and the edge is whole again, a corner of a box is cut off by a triangle. The points after it are numbered one lower. A removal that leaves no solid (the block would have fewer than four points or four faces, the faces would cross) is refused with a toast and the block stays as it was, and a block with coordinates bound to variables keeps its points ("Unbind the points of the block first"). A box is turned into a block by the press, as for the other modes.
- **Flat faces** (option **Faces**, **Free** by default): with **Flat** the point being dragged stays in the planes of the faces that have it, so every face of four or more corners stays flat. A face's plane is that of its other corners (the best fit when there are more than three); a triangle sets no plane, and corners on a line set none. A corner of a box lies in three planes and cannot move, a point on an edge slides along the edge, a point inside a face of a block with a bent surface moves in the plane. The readout says "(flat)"; the grid and the guides still apply before the planes, and the Free placement leaves the grid but not the planes. With **Free** a face that bends is built from triangles.
- **Option disc:** from the top, clockwise: **Move point**, **Split edge**, **Remove point**, a gap, **Free faces**, **Flat faces**, and two sections without a function. The grid is the one of the Settings panel. The click of the stick puts the mode and the faces back.
- **Point values:** the point that was pressed last is the picked point of its block, and so is the point that lies at a vertex chosen with the Select tool (target Vertex; found by its place, since the numbers of the vertices of the shape are not those of the points). While the block is selected the value panels show **Point X, Point Y and Point Z** of that point before the position of the block (the point is in the placement of the block), so a coordinate is set with a slider, the stick or the number pad like any value, and the variables work on it: **Make variable** and a typed expression such as `point_z / 2` bind the coordinate to the `Variables` sheet. FreeCAD cannot bind one element of a list, so the first binding of a point makes a vector property `Point<i>` on the block that carries the expressions and the point takes its value. A bound coordinate is held by its expression: the sliders, the stick and a typed number change the variable when the coordinate is bound to one variable alone (and are refused otherwise), and dragging the point with the Points tool is refused with a readout that names the expression, until the coordinate is unbound.

### Sketch tool

The sixth tool of the palette draws sketches in space (`tools/sketch.py`, `cad/sketching.py`, `cad/sketchinfer.py`). A sketch is a real `Sketcher::SketchObject` with real constraints, so it can be opened and edited in FreeCAD, extruded (Shape panel) and used by any other feature.

- **Current sketch:** the sketch that is selected. The tool selects the sketch it draws in, so drawing goes on in it; selecting anything else or choosing another plane on the option disc ends it. With no current sketch the first press makes a new one: **on the flat face** the tip touches (the face is highlighted; the sketch is attached to it with map mode FlatFace, so it follows the face when the solid changes), else **on a plane** of the document, XY, XZ or YZ, at the height of the cursor rounded to the grid. A square ten grid steps wide around the cursor shows the plane of a new sketch. In a sketch the cursor is the point of its plane that is nearest to the tip, rounded to the grid (the Free placement leaves it free), so the hand may be anywhere in front of the plane.
- **Draw** (modes **Line**, **Rectangle** and **Circle**, default Rectangle): the trigger pressed at one point, dragged and released at another draws the shape; the preview outline and the readout ("Rectangle: 50 mm x 30 mm", "Circle: radius 20 mm", "Line: 40 mm") follow the hand. A tap, or a drag that leaves no length (a rectangle needs both sides), draws nothing and makes no empty sketch. One drawing is one undo step, a sketch made with its first shape included. A grip cancels.
- **Arc** (mode **Arc**): the drag gives the two ends of the arc (the preview is the chord, the readout "Arc: chord 60 mm"), and the release does not finish it: the third point then follows the hand (by the placement), the preview is the arc through the two ends and that point and the readout "Arc: radius 30 mm", and the next press of the trigger draws it, all in one undo step. Three points on a line make no arc (the toast says so and the third point goes on following); a grip cancels; a tap draws nothing.
- **Constraints:** a new shape gets the constraints that it implies (`cad/sketchinfer.py`): a line along an axis of the sketch is horizontal or vertical, a rectangle is four lines joined end to start, two horizontal and two vertical, and an arc has its two ends tied the same way (its centre and radius are free), an end or a corner that lies on a point of the sketch is **coincident** with it (the origin included), on a line or a circle it is **on that object**, on an axis of the sketch it is **on the axis**, a circle centre likewise. A line or an arc that joins the end of an arc (or an arc that joins the end of a line) and runs along it there is **tangent** to it (an end-to-end tangent, which includes the join; two lines are never made tangent), and a line that is neither horizontal nor vertical and runs exactly along or across a slanted line of the sketch is **parallel** or **perpendicular** to the first such line, and a line exactly as long as another line is **equal** to the first such line (it follows when that line is dimensioned or changed), as long as the constraints leave nothing redundant (a line tied at both ends gets no such relation). The relations are exact: a line on the grid that is a little off gets none. At most one constraint is made per point, and a constraint that the solver does not accept (redundant or conflicting) is left out and the shape stays; the toast tells how many were kept ("Rectangle drawn, 8 constraints"). Dimensions are not made by drawing, so the shape can be dragged into shape.
- **Edit:** the trigger pressed on a **point** of a sketch (every point is marked with an orange cross, the point under the cursor is highlighted blue within 12 mm) drags it: the point is moved and the solver finds the nearest sketch that keeps the constraints, so the corner of a rectangle drags the two sides it joins, a constrained point may stop short or not move, and a move the solver cannot take is refused with a readout. Release keeps it in one undo step, a grip cancels. Pressed on a **line** (within 20 mm) it gives that a **named dimension**, its length as it is now ("Dimension length = 80 mm", names `length`, `length2`), in one undo step; a line that is dimensioned already says so. Pressed on a **circle or an arc** (within 20 mm) it **drags the radius**: the radius gets its dimension if it has none (named `radius`, `radius2`), and once the hand has moved 12 mm of stage from the press the radius follows the distance of the hand from the centre (the change of that distance is the change of the radius), rounded to the grid (at least one grid step; the Free placement leaves it off the grid), and the readout says "Radius: 50 mm". The dimension, if it was made by the press, and the drag are one undo step; release keeps it ("Radius 40 mm", or the toast of the new dimension when the hand did not move), a tap on a circle that is dimensioned already changes nothing and says so, a radius the solver cannot take is refused with a readout and stays at the last good one, a grip cancels.
- **Dimensions are values:** while a sketch is selected the value panels list its named dimensions (and its position when it is not attached to a face), so a dimension is set with a slider, the stick or the number pad, and can be bound to a variable or to an expression like any value (`Constraints.<name>` in FreeCAD); the solver moves the sketch to the nearest one that keeps the other constraints and a value it cannot take is refused ("The sketch cannot take that value").
- **Option disc:** eight sections, from the top clockwise: **Rectangle**, **Circle**, **Line**, **Arc**, **Edit**, **XY plane**, **XZ plane**, **YZ plane**; the click of the stick does nothing. Choosing a plane ends the current sketch, so the next new sketch lies on that plane (default XY); the middle of the disc names the grid and the mode with its plane.

### Tool options

A tool declares its options (`ToolOption` in `tools/base.py`): a number with a range and a step, a length in millimetres (shown in the unit system of the document) or a choice from a list. While such a tool is active the stick of the tool hand belongs to them, and there are two ways to use it:

- **The option disc** (the Create, Select, Move, Points and Sketch tool, `Tool.disc_slots`): the stick chooses among **eight sections** like a clock (every disc and the quick radial have eight; a tool with fewer choices leaves sections without a function), the first straight up and then clockwise, drawn as a disc of segments attached to the tool hand: centred over the thumbstick of the controller model, in the plane of the top of the controller, 4 cm above the stick, turning with the controller like the carousel (the same look as the quick radial, 7 cm radius). A controller model without a thumbstick part gets the disc at a point above the controller. The grid and the angle step are not options of a tool: they are the sliders of the Settings panel, the same for every tool. The direction of the stick points at a section (it lights up blue) and pushing the stick out applies it: a section that sets an option (a shape, a target, a move mode) does so once and is lit orange while it holds the current value; a section that toggles an option (Remove and Add of the Select tool) does the same and, when the option already has its value, puts it back to its default; a section that steps an option steps at once and repeats after 0.3 s, ten steps per second, while the stick is held out and stops at the ends of the option. Rolling the stick round the rim applies the next section. The middle of the disc shows the current values. The disc is shown while the stick is out and for 1.5 s after a change, and not at all while a grip holds the world; while it is shown the laser or the tip of the tool hand is hidden, so the pointer never covers the text in the middle. The click of the stick goes to the tool (Select: target and operation back to their defaults; Points: mode and faces back; Create, Sketch and Move: nothing).
- **Two axes** (a tool without a disc): the stick up and down changes the first option, left and right the second. A number steps once when the stick is pushed out and repeats while it is held; a choice steps once per push and wraps round. The click puts the option changed last (the first when none was changed) back to its default, and the readout on the wrist shows the option and its value ("Grid: 10 mm") for 1.5 s.

The options are inactive while a grip holds the world and while the quick radial is open. A tool without options leaves the stick unused.

### Value panels

While an editable object is selected (by any tool, or on the desktop), the **values carousel** (`ui/value_module.py`, `core/values.py`, `cad/values.py`, `cad/variables.py`) replaces the palette carousel on the palette hand (the left hand). There is no editing mode and no input that opens it: selecting an object brings the values carousel up at its front side (the hand gives a short tick), selecting another object rebinds the panels (the carousel that is shown stays), and clearing the selection swaps the palette carousel back, turned to the side it was left at. The **trigger of the palette hand** swaps the two carousels while an object is selected, so a tool is chosen without clearing the selection; each carousel comes back turned to the side it was left at, and a toast says "Values carousel" or "Tools carousel". The trigger swaps nothing while the active tool is in a gesture (the Move tool scales a grabbed object with the trigger of the other hand) and while a grip holds the world. Every tool keeps working as usual. The panels are ordinary depth-tested panels of a carousel, so they are looked at, hovered, pressed and dragged with the laser or the tip like the others; like the others they are seen when the head looks at the left controller.

The **Value** panel is fixed: it stands at the top of every side of the values carousel, so the readout and the sliders are in view whichever side is turned to the front. It cannot be lifted and nothing can be dropped on it. The other panels (Values, Number pad, Keys, Variables) are rearranged like the panels of the palette (see Rearranging panels: lift a panel by its title bar, drop it next to or above or below another one, or on the empty side); their arrangement is saved in `%APPDATA%\BlocksCAD\values_layout.json`, and the **Reset layout** face restores it together with the palette layout. A side that loses its last panel disappears with its Value panel, and a panel dropped on the empty side gets a Value panel of its own. The faces of the values carousel cannot be lifted or hidden. To begin with the front side holds the Values panel, and the next side the Number pad over Keys over Variables.

From the top of a side, in the order the eye follows: what is edited, how it is set, the keys, the rest.

- **Value** (fixed, top row of every side): three whole rows. The **readout**, which is also the text field of the keys, shows the picked value large, with its name above it ("Length", and "Width = length * 2" when the value is bound to an expression); while text is typed it shows the text with a cursor on an orange plate, the name line reads "Length, Enter applies", and the value comes back when Enter or Clear ends it. Below it the **value slider** sets the picked value; its label gives the two ends of its track ("0 to 200 mm", "-100 to 100 mm"). The track runs from 0 (a size) or from minus its top (a position) to a round number (1, 2, 5, 10, 20 ... in millimetres) that is at least twice the value and ten steps; the range grows to the next round number when a value outside it is typed or the step changes, never while a slider is dragged. The **step slider** sets the step (see Step). The title names the object.
- **Values** (the front side to begin with): twelve faces that list every value of the object with its number ("Length 120 mm", "X 0 mm"), in the order of the selector. A press picks that value, and the face of the picked value is lit orange; the list follows the selector, the stick and the keys, and the numbers follow the object. An object with more than twelve values (a sketch with many dimensions) shows eleven of them and a last face "More values", which turns to the next page of values; the list shows the page of the picked value whenever another value is picked by other means.
- **Number pad**: 7 8 9 Del, 4 5 6 Enter, 1 2 3 Unit. What is typed shows on the readout.
- **Keys**: 0 . - +, * / ( ), and a last row with the **selector** and **Clear**: **Previous value**, a cell that says which value is picked ("Value 2 of 6", it only shows and is never lit by the pointer), **Next value**, **Clear**. The selector goes round the ends; for an object with one value Previous and Next show a dash and do nothing.
- **Variables**: eight faces that each show a variable of the document with its length ("length 120 mm", "-" where there is none), **Make variable**, **Unbind** and **More variables** (the next eight). See Variables below.
- **What the values are:** the size properties of a primitive (Length, Width, Height; Radius; Radius 1 and Radius 2; X size, Y size, Z size and Top X size of a wedge; the Length of an extrusion; the Radius of a fillet and the Length of a chamfer), the dimensions of a sketch (see Sketch tool), then the position X, Y and Z of any object that has a placement (the base of the `Placement`, document millimetres), shown in the measurement system of the document. One value is **picked** at a time, the first when an object is selected; the Values panel and the selector pick another, and the readout, the value slider, the stick and the number pad all work on the picked value. The values are read again every frame, so they follow the object when it is moved or edited elsewhere.
- **Focus:** the value slider is focused as described under Sliders: the tip within a hand's breadth in front of the panel or the laser over the row. Focusing keeps the stick of the tool hand for the panels for 0.6 s after the pointer has left it (the step slider is never focused).
- **Changing a value:** by dragging the bar of the value slider as described under Sliders; the value follows the pointer along the track in whole steps. Or, while the value slider is focused, by the **stick of the tool hand**: up and down change the picked value by one step, to the next multiple of the step (from 23 mm with a step of 10 mm to 30 mm), and repeat ten times a second after 0.3 s held out. While the value slider is focused the stick belongs to the value panels (the option disc of the tool stays away); without a focused slider it belongs to the tool as usual. A grip makes the stick the world's.
- **Step:** one of 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 and 100 mm (a logarithmic scale), for the stick and the value slider. The **step slider** sets it: its track is the thirteen steps spread evenly, its label names the step ("Step 10 mm"), and a drag moves to the nearest one. It starts as the grid of the Move tool when the panels first appear (10 mm when another tool is active; the nearest step on the scale) and keeps its value from one object to the next.
- **Typing:** the keys build a text, which the readout shows; **Enter** applies it to the picked value. A number without a unit is in the unit of the document's measurement system (mm, cm, m or inch). Text such as `2*(3 cm+4)` is evaluated: numbers and lengths add (a plain number is in the default unit), a length is multiplied or divided by a number, and the units are mm, cm, m, in and ft; **Unit** turns the unit at the end of the text through mm, cm, m and in; **Del** removes the last character and **Clear** all of it. Text that cannot be read stays and a toast says why. The result is written to the object as a number, unless the text uses a variable, in which case the value is bound to an expression (see Variables).
- **Undo:** every gesture is one undo step of its own (a slider drag from press to release, one push of the stick from out to centre, one Enter, one Make variable, one Unbind), so `button2` takes them back one by one.
- **Limits:** sizes are at least 0.1 mm (the radius of the top of a cone and the top width of a wedge may be zero). A feature that cannot be built with a value (a torus whose tube is wider than its ring) keeps the old value, a toast says so, and the same holds for a slider.
- **With the tools:** the panels do not take the trigger except over their own faces; the stick steps values only while the tool is not busy with the document (a grab); a slider that is not drawn (the object has fewer values) does nothing.

### Variables

A variable is a named length of the document that values are bound to by expressions, so changing it changes everything bound to it (`cad/variables.py`, `core/values.py`). The variables are the rows of a spreadsheet named `Variables` that is made with the first variable: column A holds the name, column B the length with the name as its alias, so a value is bound to `Variables.width` and the sheet can be edited on the desktop like any other. Names are lower case letters, digits and underscores, made from the label of the value ("length", "radius_1", "position_x"; a number is added when the name is taken).

- **Make variable** turns the picked value into a variable and binds the value to it. When a number is typed, that number is the variable (and the new value); when an expression is typed, nothing is made (a toast says so). A value that is bound to a variable already says so. The variable and its binding are one undo step.
- **Typing a variable:** pressing a face of the Variables panel types the name into the text of the Number pad (a space goes before it after a digit or a letter). **Enter** then evaluates the text with the variables as lengths: `width * 2 + 10 mm` is a length, and a text that uses a variable binds the picked value to the expression (the toast shows it, "Width = (length * 2) + 10 mm"); FreeCAD keeps it as an expression of the property, so it follows the variable. A name that is not a variable is refused ("Unknown variable 'height'"). A plain number next to a length is in the unit of the document.
- **Bound values:** a slider whose value is bound shows the expression after the value ("Width 250 mm = length * 2 + 10 mm"). When a value is bound to **one variable alone**, the sliders, the stick and a typed plain number change that variable, so everything bound to it follows; when it is bound to any other expression they are refused with a toast ("Bound to length * 2: unbind it first"). **Unbind** frees the picked value from its expression and it keeps the number it has.
- **Position:** a bound position is held by its expression, so the Move tool does not move an object along a bound axis (the expression restores it) until the value is unbound.
- **Limits:** a variable that the shape cannot take (a length of zero for a size) is refused and the old value stays; the panel reads the variables of the document again every half second.
- A variable can be bound to any length of the panels; a count (the number of a pattern) is not a length and cannot be bound.

### Undo and redo

`button2` of the tool hand outside navigation: a **tap** undoes (when the button is released within 0.4 s), a **hold** of 0.4 s redoes once while the button is still down. While a grip holds the world `button2` is the 1 : 1 lock, and a grip pressed while it is down cancels the undo. Both show a toast ("Undo", "Redo", "Nothing to undo", "Nothing to redo").

### Toasts, tooltips and readouts

All three are dark plates that fit their text, drawn over the model in the interface font.

- **Toasts** hang on the wrist of the tool hand controller: 7 cm behind the face buttons (towards the wrist) and 3 cm above their plane, tracked on the controller and always turned to face the head, at 0.6 times the size of the labels and stacked 2.8 cm apart. While the tool hand is not tracked they stand 0.8 m in front of the head, 0.2 m below the line of sight, at 1.5 times the size of the labels, and stack 6 cm apart. Up to three stand at once, the newest at the bottom; each stays for 2.2 s, grows in and shrinks away, and a message that is still there is renewed instead of repeated. Toasts report the result of an action: undo and redo, the tool chosen with its hint, the pointer mode, layout reset, hidden and shown faces, the selection and deletion of objects.
- **Tooltips:** hovering one face for 0.5 s shows its description above it, 1.9 cm in front of its panel (towards the head) and parallel to it. Tool faces use the hint of the tool. The text is broken into lines of about 36 characters and the plate is as wide as its longest line (every label plate is as wide as its measured text plus a margin). The tooltip disappears when the pointer leaves the face and is not shown while something is lifted, pressed or a slider is dragged. The digit and symbol keys of the value panels (0 to 9, point, minus, plus, times, divide, brackets) have none.
- **Readout** of a running gesture (the move of an object, the size of a shape being dragged out, the distance of a push) and of a tool option (see Tool options): an orange plate on the wrist like a toast, at the same place and size, at the bottom of the stack with the toasts one place above it. It never stands next to the pointer, so it does not cover the object that is worked on. While the tool hand is not tracked it stands in front of the head like the toasts.

### Controls card

What the buttons do is shown on the controllers themselves (`ui/help.py`, `ui/feedback_view.py`). The **Tutorial** face of the Settings panel toggles a **card** over each controller: a plate with a title (orange) and one line per control ("Trigger: ...") on plates of one width, 15 cm above the controller, turned to face the head, at the size of the toasts on the wrist, drawn over the model like the other messages.

- **Tool hand card:** titled "<tool>: tool hand". It lists what the active tool declares in `Tool.controls` (the trigger, where it matters the A button and what the click of the stick does, for example "Trigger: tap: select, hold: move and turn", "A: trigger held: snap, grid, free"), then "A: hold: quick menu" when the tool says nothing about A, and what every tool has: "Stick: options of the tool", "B: tap: undo, hold: redo", "Grip: grab the world (both: turn, scale)". The card follows the active tool.
- **Palette hand card:** "Palette hand": the stick turns the palette, the trigger swaps the palette carousel and the values carousel, the grips, A pins the palette, the D-pad undoes and redoes, the menu button shows or hides the card.
- **Menu button:** the menu button of either controller (where it has one) toggles the cards at any time, whatever the carousel shows.
- **Time:** the cards go after 40 s or when the face is pressed again (toasts say "Controls shown" and "Controls hidden"). A hand that is not tracked has no card.
- **First run:** the first time the addon runs (the setting `onboarded` in `settings.json` is false) the cards show by themselves with a toast that names the face; when they go, by time or by the face, `onboarded` is saved and they stay away from then on.

### Quick radial

A pin-cushion like Open Brush's: holding tool hand `button1` (outside navigation) opens a radial of the favourite faces around the controller.

- **Items:** the eight faces activated last (from the carousel or the radial), newest first, padded from the defaults (Undo, Redo, Reset view, Delete, Move, Pointer, Copy, Colour) when fewer than eight were used. Hidden faces, sliders, Reset layout, Close VR and the Select tool (the hub item) are never offered. The list is kept in `settings.json`.
- **Choosing:** the item is chosen by the **direction the hand moves** from the spot where the button went down, seen from the head and measured in the plane facing the head (no stick needed). The first item is straight up, the others follow clockwise. Nothing is chosen within 3 cm of the start (dead zone). A haptic tick marks every change of the choice; the chosen segment lights up and a dot follows the hand over the disc.
- **Hub item:** the middle of the radial holds the **Select** tool. It is lit (blue) while the hand is inside the dead zone and its name is written on it; releasing the button there, so pressing and releasing A without moving the hand, chooses the Select tool, with the same tick as any other choice. The hub item is not a favourite: it is never remembered or drawn as a segment, and it stays in the hub when its face is hidden.
- **Release** of the button activates the chosen item, or the hub item when no segment is chosen; starting a grip cancels.
- **Drawing:** a disc of 12.7 cm radius cut into one pie segment per item (segments from 3 cm to 11.5 cm, thin gaps between them), with the label of the face on each segment and a hub of 3 cm radius in the middle, which is the dead zone and holds the hub item. It stands 5 cm in front of the hand towards the head, drawn over the model, in the colours and font of the palette. The plane is fixed when the radial opens.
- **Blocked:** the radial does not open while a grip holds the world (there `button1` is the level modifier), while a slider is dragged, a panel or a face is lifted, a face is pressed, or the active tool runs a gesture (a grab, a shape being dragged out: there `button1` locks the grab to the grid). A grip pressed while it is open cancels it. While it is open the tool hand's pointer does not touch the carousel and its trigger belongs to the interface.

## Palette hand

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Stick left/right | turn the carousel |
| Stick up/down | page scroll for long panels |
| Trigger | swap the palette carousel and the values carousel while an object is selected (not while the active tool is in a gesture: the Move tool scales with it) |
| Grip | grab the world (alone or with the other grip) |
| Button 1 | pin or release the carousel |
| `aux1` (Steam Frame: D-pad left) | undo |
| `aux2` (Steam Frame: D-pad right) | redo |

Controllers without `aux1` and `aux2` undo and redo through the Edit panel faces; the tool hand shortcuts on `button2` cover every profile.

## Visual style

Flat, high contrast panels: dark plate (RGB 0.10, 0.11, 0.14), face plate (0.20, 0.22, 0.28), hover (0.30, 0.45, 0.80), active tool and lifted panel (0.95, 0.65, 0.10), drop target (0.18, 0.36, 0.24), text white.
Every panel of the carousel has a tint, so the panels tell apart at a glance: Tools green, Edit rose, View ochre, Modify violet, Settings teal, Pointer lime, any other panel one of five further hues chosen by its name. A tint is the grey of the default panel (plate, title bar, face, empty slot, lit plate) turned to the hue at the same luminance, so white text, the hover blue and the active orange keep exactly the contrast they have on the grey panels (11.7 : 1 for the text on a face, 2.6 : 1 for the hover on a face). The panels of the value module stay grey.
A colour node is written only when its colour changes, and the contents of a panel that is seen from behind are not drawn (its plate hides them), so a carousel that is shown costs about as little as a hidden one.
Text is drawn from glyph outlines (Coin `SoText3`) in Segoe UI Bold (the platform default where the family is missing) at fixed height and sits on a plate so scene geometry never overlaps it.
The colours, the font and the text size are defined once in `ui/style.py`. Controllers keep the runtime-provided models.
